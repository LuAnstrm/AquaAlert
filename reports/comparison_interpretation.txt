SECTION 2 – WHY ACCURACY IS NOT THE RIGHT PRIMARY METRIC
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Accuracy measures the fraction of all predictions that are correct. In a
balanced dataset this is a useful summary, but the AquaAlert dataset is
imbalanced: High-risk events make up only approximately 6 % of
observations. A model that labels every observation as 'Low' would
achieve very high accuracy while detecting zero floods. For a flood
warning system, the cost of a missed flood (a false negative) is
incomparably higher than the cost of a false alarm (a false positive),
so accuracy alone is a misleading guide.

SECTION 3 – THE DANGER OF FALSE NEGATIVES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A FALSE NEGATIVE occurs when the model predicts Low or Moderate risk but
the true risk level is High. In the context of AquaAlert, a false
negative means: barangay residents are not warned, evacuation is not
triggered, and people remain in flood-prone areas during a dangerous
event. The direct consequence could be loss of life, injuries, or
preventable damage to property.

In the current test set, Logistic Regression missed 0 of 6 truly High-
risk events (High-recall = 1.00). Random Forest missed 2 of 6 (High-
recall = 0.67). Each missed event represents a potential failure to warn
a community.

SECTION 6 – INTERPRETABILITY
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Logistic Regression is the more interpretable model: each feature has a
single coefficient per class that shows its direction and magnitude of
influence. A barangay official can be shown 'rainfall in the last 3
hours has the strongest positive effect on High-risk probability.'
Random Forest offers feature importances (how often each feature reduced
prediction error) but individual predictions are harder to explain
because they aggregate 100–300 decision trees.
